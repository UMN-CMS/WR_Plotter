\begin{frame}{Binned-measurement resolution: $\sigma = \Delta x / \sqrt{12}$}

  \textbf{Setup.} A particle (or event) lands somewhere in a bin of width
  $\Delta x$ centered at $x_0$. We report $x_0$ as the measured position.
  Without extra information, the true position is uniformly distributed
  inside the bin:
  \[
    x_{\mathrm{true}} \sim \mathcal{U}(x_0 - \Delta x/2,\ x_0 + \Delta x/2).
  \]
  The residual $r \equiv x_{\mathrm{true}} - x_0$ has
  $r \sim \mathcal{U}(-\Delta x/2,\ +\Delta x/2)$, with $\langle r \rangle = 0$.

  \textbf{Variance.}
  \[
    \operatorname{Var}[r]
      = \mathbb{E}[r^{2}]
      = \int_{-\Delta x/2}^{+\Delta x/2} r^{2}\,\frac{1}{\Delta x}\,dr
      = \frac{1}{\Delta x}\left[\frac{r^{3}}{3}\right]_{-\Delta x/2}^{+\Delta x/2}
      = \frac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{12}.
  \]

  \textbf{Standard deviation (the ``resolution'').}
  \[
    \boxed{\,\sigma = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[r]} = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\sqrt{12}}
    \ \approx\ \dfrac{\Delta x}{3.46}.\,}
  \]

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \textbf{Why it's $\Delta x / \sqrt{12}$, not $\Delta x$:} we know the particle
  hit \emph{inside} the bin, not only somewhere within $\pm \Delta x$. The
  RMS of a uniform distribution is the natural error bar for a binned measurement.

  \textbf{Application to the on-shell FWHM.} Each half-max crossing
  (left and right of the peak) has this intrinsic floor; the two crossings
  are independent, so their variances add:
  \[
    \sigma_{\mathrm{FWHM}}^{\mathrm{bin}}
      = \sqrt{2}\,\frac{\Delta m}{\sqrt{12}}
      \ \overset{\Delta m\,=\,60\,\mathrm{GeV}}{=}\ 24.5\ \mathrm{GeV}.
  \]
  Added in quadrature with the Poisson bootstrap uncertainty to give the
  total per-point FWHM error bar.

\end{frame}
